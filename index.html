ABOUT ZANE

Meet the young karateka behind the journey.

What began as something fun at age five has grown into a journey of training, competition, confidence and a dream of one day representing the Philippines.

Hi, I'm Zane.

I am an aspiring karateka, with dreams of joining the Philippine Karate National Team and representing our country one day.

HOW IT STARTED

As a kid, I was totally obsessed with Power Rangers and Ninja Kids! I couldn't help but copy their epic moves and pretend to be a superhero.

One day, my “Yoya” — my lola — caught me in action and asked, “How about giving karate a shot?”

On the morning of July 22, 2023, I eagerly joined my first karate class. I jumped in like a ninja on a mission — and I've been loving every kick, punch and lesson ever since.

Since then, every class, belt promotion and tournament has taught me something new. There have been wins, losses, medals and difficult matches, but every experience has helped me grow — and I am still only at the beginning of my journey.

WHY THIS STAGE MATTERS

Before the world knew their names, some Filipino champions competed here too.

Batang Pinoy was created as a bridge from grassroots sport to higher levels of competition. For Zane, Bacolod is not a promise of where his journey will end — it is a chance to gain the same kind of national experience that has helped shape generations of Filipino athletes.

WEIGHTLIFTING

Hidilyn Diaz-Naranjo

Batang Pinoy 2002 → Olympic Champion

Her first competition was the 2002 Batang Pinoy, where she won gold. She later became the Philippines' first Olympic gold medalist and also won Olympic silver.

GYMNASTICS

Carlos Yulo

Grassroots pathway → 2× Olympic Gold

The Philippine Sports Commission has cited Batang Pinoy and the Philippine National Games among the grassroots competitions that helped produce Yulo, who later won two Olympic gold medals in Paris 2024.

BOXING

Carlo Paalam

Batang Pinoy pathway → Olympic Silver

Paalam rose through Batang Pinoy and other national youth competitions before becoming an Olympic silver medalist for the Philippines.

BOXING

Eumir Marcial

Batang Pinoy alumnus → Olympic Bronze

Marcial is a former Batang Pinoy and Philippine National Games competitor who went on to win an Olympic bronze medal and major international boxing honors.

WUSHU

Agatha Wong

2× Batang Pinoy Gold → World & Asian Medalist

Wong won two Batang Pinoy gold medals in 2013 before becoming a six-time SEA Games champion, World Wushu silver medalist and Asian Games bronze medalist.

SAMBO

Sydney Sy-Tancontian

9× Batang Pinoy Gold → World Sambo Medalist

After collecting nine Batang Pinoy gold medals from 2011 to 2016, Sy-Tancontian went on to earn medals at the World Sambo Championship and other international events.

THE POINT IS NOT THAT EVERY YOUNG ATHLETE BECOMES AN OLYMPIAN.

It is that national youth competition can be an important place to learn, be tested and discover how far an athlete can grow. For Zane, Batang Pinoy 2026 is another opportunity to take that step.

THE STANDARD BEHIND THE GEAR

Built for recognized competition standards.

Karate Pilipinas Sports Federation is the Philippine Olympic Committee's member federation for karate and the country's national federation recognized by the World Karate Federation. Batang Pinoy's published karate technical rules apply updated WKF rules and require approved uniforms and protective equipment.

For a competitor, that means the correct approval matters at equipment check. The goal is not to buy the most expensive gear — it is to make sure Zane has the correct, competition-legal equipment for Kata and Kumite.

We will still confirm the final Batang Pinoy 2026 technical bulletin, Zane's size and the required approval markings with his coaches and tournament officials before purchase.

Karate GiBatang Pinoy's published rules call for WKF- or Karate Pilipinas-approved Aka / Ao competition uniforms for Kata and Kumite.
Body protectionApproved body protection is compulsory for Kumite. Current WKF rules also require an external body protector for athletes under 14.
Shin + instepApproved red and blue shin and instep protection is required for Kumite competition.
Mitts + beltsApproved red and blue mitts and red / blue belts (obi) are part of the published Batang Pinoy equipment requirements.
Under-14 helmetCurrent 2026 WKF Kumite rules require a WKF-approved helmet for athletes under 14.
